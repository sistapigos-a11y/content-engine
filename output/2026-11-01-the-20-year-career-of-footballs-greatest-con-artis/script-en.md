He navigated the elite youth systems of the city, passing through the academies of both Botafogo and Flamengo. It was during this brief stint in Mexico that Carlos arrived at a bizarre, life-altering epiphany. He adored the perks of the profession: the status, the nightlife, and the adulation of the fans. He desperately wanted the life of a professional footballer—he just happened to hate playing football.

This was the golden age of the 'eye test.' Without modern digital scouting, YouTube highlights, or global databases, a player’s reputation was built on hearsay and physical presence. Kaiser realized that if he carried himself with the confidence of a champion, coaches would simply assume the talent was there, buried under a temporary lack of match fitness.

*[photo: Carlos Kaiser (footballer)]*

He returned to Brazil and began his career as a 'farce footballer,' using his charisma to build a massive network of legendary friends like Carlos Alberto Torres and Renato Gaúcho. *[photo: Carlos Kaiser (footballer)]*

Upon arrival at legendary clubs like Botafogo or Flamengo, his strategy never wavered. He would report to the managers and claim he lacked 'match fitness,' requesting weeks of isolation to get his body ready.

*[broll: athlete track running slow motion]*

This allowed him to dodge the pitch and spend his days in purely physical training. On the running track, his raw speed and elite physique were undeniable, convincing everyone he was a superstar in waiting.
