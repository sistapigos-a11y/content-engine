## 3. The $600 Breakfast

*[photo: Jensen Huang]*

By 1993, Jensen Huang had established himself as a talented microchip designer in Silicon Valley. But working inside major corporations left him restless, yearning to build something of his own.

*[broll: vintage 1990s computer motherboards chips]*

He crossed paths with two frustrated engineers, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem. While collaborating on graphics hardware, they shared a massive vision: building a dedicated chip that could render real-time 3D graphics for personal computers.

*[broll: retro diner booth interior at night]*

To hatch their plan, the trio met frequently at a roadside Denny's diner in East San Jose. Jensen chose the restaurant because of his past graveyard-shift work there, knowing it was quieter than home and offered cheap coffee.

*[text: $600 STARTUP CAPITAL]*

Over a breakfast booth, the three men officially incorporated their venture. They pulled out their wallets and scraped together just six hundred dollars in total capital, two hundred dollars from each founder's pocket.

*[text: NVIDIA]*

They initially called the company NVision, before settling on Nvidia, derived from the Latin word invidia, meaning envy. Curtis Priem wanted their future rivals to turn green with envy.

*[photo: THIRTY DAYS FROM DEATH]*

They were ready to make the world green with envy, but within years, they were thirty days away from losing everything.

*[broll: server room flashing blue lights dark]*
